# A quick tour: rethink a recurring meeting

[日本語](quick-tour.ja.md)

This condensed, fictional example illustrates the workflow. It is not a real-user transcript or a test result.

**User:** Our weekly meeting is a round of updates, yet changes to handoff dates still catch people out. Think about it from zero.

**Assistant:** My tentative understanding is that you want people to hear about changes early enough to adjust their work. Is that the value to preserve, or would you put it differently?

**User:** Yes, with one correction: hearing about a change is not enough. The person waiting for the handoff needs to agree to the new date. We report dates now, but never check that agreement. The owners are already clear.

**Assistant:** Then the purpose is to agree on changed handoff dates while there is still time to adjust. We'll keep the existing responsibilities. Assuming teammates can contact each other during the week, here are three ways to achieve that from a blank sheet:

| Approach | What people do | Benefit and burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct agreement | The sender proposes a date and explains the impact to the receiver, who agrees or flags a conflict | Only the people affected need to act; unanswered requests need follow-up |
| A shared handoff list | People record proposed dates in one shared list; receivers accept them or request a change | Dependencies are visible together; the list must stay current and be checked |
| A regular handoff review | People with unresolved handoffs meet to agree dates and record the result | Connected conflicts can be discussed together; changes may wait for the slot |

Your current meeting is closest to the third approach because it uses a regular group conversation. Its update round leaves agreement on each handoff unconfirmed.

I would first try direct agreement on one changed handoff. Check whether the receiver had enough notice and could agree to the date. Whether a shared list or a discussion is also needed remains open.
