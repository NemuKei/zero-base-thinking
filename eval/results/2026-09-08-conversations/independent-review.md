This review uses the final setup.json supplied with the completed proposal pair; it supersedes the earlier incomplete-metadata finding. References such as U3 and A3 mean the user and assistant messages with `turn: 3` in the named transcript. This review evaluates the recorded dialogue, not the runtime's implementation or real-user usefulness.

The six conversations reach a useful provisional direction and complete the ordinary edit. I found no material conversational failure requiring a runtime change. Both recovery arms provide substantive alternatives after the user's revised account of the purpose. Both proposal arms honor the request for options first and adapt to the later delivery constraint. **purpose-recovery-control — pass, with a well-specified personal trial.** A1 suspends the old record-keeping objective and asks about participant benefit. In response to U2, A2 gives concrete examples that distinguish recording activity from its possible value; it explicitly labels the examples as unconfirmed. After U3 identifies workplace application and discomfort with being evaluated, A3 changes the objective and proposes private reflection with optional sharing. A3 asks for a concrete learning example, but when U4 requests comparisons first, A4 supplies them without repeating that question: a private practice notebook, a work-problem consultation group, and integration into ordinary work without a dedicated learning-management arrangement. Their mechanisms and different burdens are explained, and A4 says the actual obstacle to trying learning at work is unknown. U5 introduces the two-week personal trial; A5 bounds it and checks actual application, effort, and perceived evaluation pressure. A6 performs the requested edit alone. The early A3 flow is a provisional proposal before the obstacle is known, not an established diagnosis; A4 keeps that uncertainty visible.

**purpose-recovery-skill — pass, with a narrower final check.** A1 asks about value rather than accepting the old objective. A2 responds to U2 with concrete mechanisms, then reformulates the still-unanswered question; this is useful repair, not an ignored-answer loop. U3 changes the account of the value, and A3 explicitly adopts workplace application while keeping other participants' preferences unknown. When U4 cannot name the obstacle and asks for options, A4 proceeds with three meaningfully different mechanisms: direct application, private planning notes, and one-person consultation. It explains what each helps and where it can fail, and compares their role with the known current features. A4 recommends a one-time private-note experiment; U5 and A5 keep the scope personal and leave group changes open. A6 completes only the edit. Two limitations remain: tolerating private reflection supports an acceptable experiment, but does not establish that writing resolves the unknown obstacle; A4-A5 appropriately leave benefit unproven. Also, A5 explicitly checks application and obstacles but does not separately revisit effort or evaluation pressure, although A4 acknowledges those risks. The control's A5 carries those concerns into the final check more explicitly. This is a coverage difference, not evidence that the skill trial caused harm or failed.

**sound-current-approach-control — pass for the practical outcome; partial for alternatives.** A1 derives two necessary functions from U1's purpose and maps the current list and chat to them. Retention is supported by that functional fit and limited recent evidence, not solely by familiarity or user insistence. It identifies the unmeasured update burden and asks a relevant question. However, it does not compare another viable delivery mechanism; dismissing unneeded reservation/payment additions is less informative than comparing how the same value could be delivered with or without the list. U2 supplies account and usage constraints and proposes continuing while asking participants once about burden. A2 accepts that bounded check, supplies a concrete question, and preserves the limitation of three successful loans. It does not treat unknown burden as absent. A3 returns the requested heading alone. The provisional retention judgment in A1 precedes the burden answer, but A1-A2 explicitly keep that uncertainty open; I do not classify this as a material premature conclusion.

**sound-current-approach-skill — pass, with broader comparison and a concrete observation plan.** A1 constructs the required path from finding a tool to agreeing handover and return, then compares asking the chat each time against a maintained list plus direct coordination. The tradeoff is specific: repeated inquiry and waiting versus list maintenance. It conditionally recommends the current arrangement and avoids generalizing the three successful loans. After U2 supplies the constraints and asks what to observe next, A2 specifies one loan through return, checking information accuracy, coordination, and maintenance effort. Its instruction to retain unobserved effort as unknown is especially clear. U3 chooses that direction; A3 closes without another question; A4 completes the heading edit alone. One irregularly occurring loan cannot establish typical burden or reveal all between-loan maintenance, but A2 explicitly treats it as bounded evidence rather than proof. The extra closing turn is not excessive interviewing.

**proposal-first-scope-control — pass, with broader operational checks.** A1 immediately supplies three distinct options as U1 requests: reference a common result from existing reports, generate required reports from that result, or consolidate reports. It explicitly protects source data, verified rules, and result correctness. Its initial preference for generation is provisional: it says this could support mandatory forms, while software feasibility, cost, and maintenance remain unknown. U2 reveals one mandatory form and dispensable internal duplication, and asks what to check in a representative case. A2 combines the two relevant paths, then specifies correctness, form compliance, internal information needs, total recurring effort, and consistency after a correction. The proposed correction uses a test copy; this does not call for changing the protected source originals. It preserves comparison artifacts and says actual submission acceptance and unrepresented exceptions remain unverified. A3 completes only the edit. The scope is useful but wider than a minimal document comparison: timing savings and regeneration consistency require a candidate process that can actually be tried. No such process is demonstrated here. This is a prospective validation plan, not proof of feasibility or savings, and the assistant does not claim otherwise.

**proposal-first-scope-skill — pass, with a deliberately bounded first check.** A1 starts from delivering one verified result and immediately presents options, without making the user restate the known purpose. Common materials, generation into required formats, and shared attachments are different delivery mechanisms with explicit acceptance and maintenance conditions. A1 distinguishes the current manual placement of figures from whether multiple reports are needed, and leaves software capabilities unknown. U2 supplies the mandatory external form, removable internal duplication, and a decision to combine generation with common internal materials; it explicitly asks to leave implementation undecided. A2 follows that scope: one representative pair of outputs, checking numerical meaning in the specified form and whether required internal numbers and explanations remain accessible. It retains unresolved information needs and separates met, unmet, and undecidable points. A3 returns only the edited sentence. This check does not measure the eventual reduction in total transfer, checking, or maintenance effort. That remains an outcome to verify later, not a demonstrated failure of this bounded first step. It would be wrong to count the shorter plan or the user's simulated decision as evidence of greater usefulness.

No assistant repeatedly demands an already answered question, ignores a request for proposals, or asserts an observed demand, effect size, software capability, or completed real-world outcome that the permitted evidence does not support. The recovery explanations are repaired once in each arm. Neither the sound nor the proposal case contains an explanation-request or changed-purpose event, so success on those behaviors is not tested there. The proposal U2 events add delivery constraints while preserving the original objective; both A2 responses use those constraints. The examples and prospective checks remain hypothetical. All six final edits contain exactly the requested visible text, with a trailing newline and no extra commentary; equality with cases.json holds after stripping that terminal whitespace.

**Comparison limits.**

- These are single synthetic samples. The recovery user's explanation request and later value discovery are preauthored events in cases.json; neither arm demonstrates spontaneous human insight. Simulated acceptance of the final direction is not human-perceived usefulness, feasibility, or successful execution.
- Separate adaptive users create consequential differences. In recovery U4, the control user defers selecting concrete learning content, while the skill user explicitly cannot identify the obstacle. Recovery control U5 chooses a two-week trial; skill U5 chooses one attempt. Sound control U2 independently proposes a one-time participant inquiry; skill U2 asks for observations during the next loan and later supplies a separate U3 decision. In proposal U2, the control user asks the assistant to specify what to check, while the skill user already selects the two checks and explicitly leaves implementation undecided. These differences partly determine checks, length, and turn count; they are not controlled outputs of the skill alone.
- protocol.md and setup.json describe fresh assistant/user roles with no copied adaptive replies or cross-arm carryover. They also state that shared host instructions and catalog exposure are uncontrolled. This control is not a bare-model ablation. The recovery and sound skill A1 messages identify zero-base-thinking, so those simulated users can infer the arm despite not receiving an arm label. The proposal skill A1 does not make that announcement.
- The final setup.json resolves the earlier missing-order finding. Its complete recorded initial assistant order is purpose-recovery-control, purpose-recovery-skill, sound-current-approach-skill, sound-current-approach-control, proposal-first-scope-control, then proposal-first-scope-skill. It states that starts were not randomized and subsequent turns were interleaved as responses became available. This supplies a complete reported launch order, not a full independently verified timing trace. Exact backend build and effective settings remain unavailable; matching inheritance is a reported condition, not independently verified equality.
- The final setup also reports one recovery skill follow-up with a mistyped file path and an explicit correct fallback in the same dispatch, while preserving the exact user message in the transcript. That is a delivery irregularity to disclose. The reviewed dialogue shows no consequent missed answer or failed recovery, but it cannot establish whether the path error added actor effort or had any other delivery-level effect. I have not inspected delivery logs or inferred an affected turn that the note does not identify.
- Offline text dialogue does not test native question UI behavior, actual project execution, or human tolerance for effort. Ordinary-edit success is a separate observed behavior, not proof of the preceding consultation's quality. Retention, replacement, shorter text, and announcing a skill are not themselves success criteria.

The evidence supports preserving the current instructions while reporting the limited alternatives in sound control and the narrower final burden check in recovery skill. It does not establish an overall improvement estimate or justify a runtime change. A claimed material defect would require a focused reproduction before recommending a change.
